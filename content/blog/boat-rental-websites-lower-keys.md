# Why Lower Keys Boat Rental Businesses Are Losing Customers to Facebook (And How to Fix It)

**Published:** March 2026
**Word Count:** ~800 words

---

Here is a search happening right now: a tourist sitting in their hotel room on Summerland Key is typing "boat rental Big Pine Key" into Google on their phone. They want a center console for the day. They have a credit card ready. They will book within the next ten minutes.

If your boat rental business shows up in those results with a real website and a way to book, you get that customer. If your digital presence is a Facebook page and a phone number, you don't. And you'll never know you lost them.

This is happening dozens of times a day across the Lower Keys. Most boat rental owners aren't losing customers because their boats are bad or their prices are wrong. They're losing customers because they're invisible at the moment the decision gets made.

---

## Why Facebook Is Not a Substitute for a Website

Facebook is where you talk to people who already know you. It is not where new customers find you.

When a tourist searches Google for a boat rental, Google returns websites — specifically websites that load fast on mobile, have booking information, and are optimized for local search. A Facebook business page ranks poorly in Google search, shows up without pricing or availability, and requires the tourist to navigate away from their search results just to understand what you offer.

There's also the algorithmic problem. Facebook shows your posts to a fraction of your followers — organic reach for business pages sits around 1–5%. The customers who liked your page three years ago probably aren't seeing your posts. The tourists who've never heard of you definitely aren't.

Word-of-mouth is valuable. Repeat customers are valuable. A Facebook page with 800 followers feels like a presence. But none of that is capturing the tourist at 9pm who doesn't know anyone in the Keys and is just trying to rent a boat for tomorrow.

That tourist goes to Google. Google sends them to a website. Your competitors with websites get booked.

## The OTA Math You Need to See

Some boat rental owners in the Lower Keys list on GetMyBoat, Boatsetter, or similar platforms to pick up online bookings. It works — but you're paying for it.

GetMyBoat and Boatsetter typically charge **20–30% per booking** in combined owner and customer fees. On a $350 day rental, that's $70–$105 disappearing to platform commission. Per booking.

Run the numbers for a full season:

- **30 bookings/month** during 5-month peak season = 150 bookings/year
- **Average day rental: $350**
- **At 25% commission: $87.50 per booking lost to the platform**
- **Total lost: ~$13,125/year**

That's the conservative case. Operators with higher rates or more volume are losing $15,000–$20,000+ annually to OTA fees. On a small-scale boat rental business, that is not a rounding error — that's the difference between a profitable season and a break-even one.

The platforms aren't evil. They delivered bookings you might not have gotten otherwise. But once a tourist finds you on GetMyBoat, they've searched for you. A direct booking website lets you capture that customer — and every customer like them — without paying a 25% toll on each transaction.

## What a Proper Website Actually Does for a Boat Rental Business

A real website for a boat rental operation in the Lower Keys isn't complicated. It's not a brochure. It's a booking machine. Here's what it needs to do:

**Show up on Google.** This means local SEO — optimizing for searches like "boat rental Big Pine Key," "center console rental Lower Keys," and "boat rental Sugarloaf Key." It means a fast-loading, mobile-first site that Google's algorithm ranks favorably. And it means a complete, verified Google Business Profile linked to the site, so you appear in the map pack when tourists search near you.

**Let people book without calling.** FareHarbor integrates directly into your site and handles your availability calendar, deposit collection, and confirmation emails. A tourist can go from your homepage to a confirmed reservation in under three minutes. No phone tag, no waiting for a callback, no lost bookings because you were on the water when someone tried to reach you.

**Show the boats, show the water.** Tourists renting a boat in the Lower Keys are buying an experience. Real photos of your boats, real shots of the backcountry flats and reef access, real images of families out on the water — these convert. A gallery of five or six genuine photos of your operation does more work than any paragraph of copy.

**Build trust on arrival.** US Coast Guard compliance, safety gear included, years in business, Google reviews — this information matters to tourists who have never met you. Surface it prominently. Families with kids are especially looking for signals that the operation is legit and the equipment is maintained.

**Show pricing clearly.** Nothing kills a booking faster than "call for rates." Show your half-day, full-day, and multi-day rates. Show fuel policy, deposit terms, and cancellation policy. Tourists comparison-shopping between two rental operations will choose the one that answers their questions without making them pick up the phone.
