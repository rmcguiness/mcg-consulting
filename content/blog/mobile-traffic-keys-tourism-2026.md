## The Mobile Reality in the Keys

Across Florida Keys tourism websites, **mobile traffic accounts for 65–70% of all visits**. That number has been climbing for years and isn't going back down. Your website's most important version isn't the one you see on your desktop at the dock office. It's the one a tourist sees on an iPhone at a restaurant in Ohio.

Google knows this too. Since 2021, Google has used **mobile-first indexing** — meaning the mobile version of your site is what Google crawls, evaluates, and ranks. If your site looks fine on desktop but breaks on mobile, Google treats it as a broken site. Period.

---

## What Bad Mobile UX Actually Costs You

It's not abstract. Bad mobile experience translates directly to lost money:

- **53% of mobile users abandon a site that takes more than 3 seconds to load.** If your site loads in 6–8 seconds (common for older WordPress themes with unoptimized images), you're losing more than half your visitors before they see anything.
- **Bounce rate = lost revenue.** Every visitor who hits "back" is a potential booking that went to a competitor. For a charter captain running $550 half-day trips, even two or three lost bookings a week adds up fast.
- **Google penalizes slow, non-responsive sites.** Poor Core Web Vitals scores push you down in search results, meaning fewer people find you in the first place.

---

## What Google Is Actually Measuring

Google evaluates mobile experience through three **Core Web Vitals**:

- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint):** How fast does the main content load? Target: under 2.5 seconds.
- **INP (Interaction to Next Paint):** How quickly does the page respond when someone taps a button? Target: under 200 milliseconds.
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift):** Does the page jump around as it loads? Target: under 0.1.

You can check your own site right now at [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). Run your URL, look at the mobile score. If it's below 70, your site is actively hurting your search ranking.

---

## Good Mobile vs. Bad Mobile — What the Difference Looks Like

**Bad mobile experience:**
- Text too small to read without zooming
- Buttons too close together (fat-finger taps hit the wrong thing)
- Images that aren't resized for mobile (huge files, slow loads)
- Horizontal scrolling required to see content
- Pop-ups that cover the screen and can't be dismissed

**Good mobile experience:**
- Full-width layout that fits the screen naturally
- Large, tappable buttons with clear labels ("Book Now," "See Trips," "Call Us")
- Images compressed and lazy-loaded (fast, but still sharp)
- Key info — pricing, availability, reviews — visible without scrolling far
- Booking flow that works entirely on mobile, start to finish

The difference between these two isn't design talent. It's whether your site was built mobile-first or desktop-first and then squeezed onto a phone.

## The Fix Isn't Complicated

A modern, mobile-first website built on a fast framework (Next.js, Astro, or even well-configured WordPress) with properly optimized images and a clean layout will score 90+ on PageSpeed and load in under 2 seconds on any phone. It doesn't require a massive budget or months of work. Most Keys tourism sites can be rebuilt in 2–3 weeks.

The question is whether you keep losing mobile visitors to a site that wasn't built for them.
