# How Vacation Rental Managers in the Florida Keys Are Losing Bookings to Outdated Websites

**Published:** March 2026
**Word Count:** ~620 words

---

There is a quiet crisis happening across the Florida Keys vacation rental industry, and most property managers don't even realize it. Their websites — the single most important tool for capturing direct bookings — are actively driving guests away.

The guests still come to the Keys. They still search Google for "vacation rental Islamorada" or "Key West waterfront house." But when they land on a site built in 2014 running a platform that hasn't been updated in years, they don't stick around. They go to VRBO. They go to Airbnb. And the property manager pays 15–20% in commission on a booking they could have captured directly.

---

## Drupal 7 Is Dead. Your Site Might Be Running on It.

In January 2025, Drupal 7 reached official end-of-life. That means no more security patches, no more bug fixes, and no more community support. Any site still running Drupal 7 is operating on borrowed time.

This isn't a theoretical risk. Several well-established Keys rental management companies are still running Drupal 7 today. Their sites load slowly, render poorly on mobile, and carry known security vulnerabilities that haven't been patched in over a year.

For a business that handles guest payment information and owner financial data, that's a serious liability — not just a bad look.

The fix isn't a Drupal upgrade. Drupal 10 is a completely different platform from Drupal 7 — there's no simple update path. Most rental managers are better served migrating to a modern stack entirely: a fast, purpose-built site with a booking integration like Lodgify, Guesty, or OwnerRez that handles availability, payments, and guest communication out of the box.

---

## Mobile Traffic Is the Majority. Most Rental Sites Ignore It.

Google's own data shows that over 60% of travel-related searches happen on mobile devices. For vacation rentals, the number is likely higher — guests are searching from airports, hotel lobbies, and poolside at their current rental while planning their next trip.

Yet many Keys rental sites were built in the responsive-design-optional era. Property galleries that don't resize. Search filters that require a desktop mouse to operate. Booking flows that break on a phone screen. Every friction point is a lost booking.

The guest doesn't call to troubleshoot. They don't switch to a laptop. They open VRBO on the same phone, search the same area, and book there instead. The property manager still gets the booking — but now VRBO takes its cut.

---

## Direct Booking vs. Platform Commission: The Math

Here's what most rental managers already know but don't act on:

- **VRBO** charges hosts 5% per booking (or 8% pay-per-booking) plus a guest service fee of 6–12%
- **Airbnb** takes 3% from the host and 14–16% from the guest
- On a $3,000 weekly rental, platform fees can exceed $400–$500 per booking

A property management company running 30–50 properties through the season can easily lose $40,000–$80,000 annually to platform commissions. A direct booking website — one that actually works on mobile, loads fast, and ranks for local search terms — typically costs $5,000–$7,500 to build.

The site pays for itself in the first handful of direct bookings. Everything after that is margin the platforms were taking.

---

## What a Modern Vacation Rental Site Needs

The bar isn't high. It just hasn't been cleared by most existing sites:

- **Sub-3-second mobile load time** — speed is both a ranking factor and a trust signal
- **Integrated booking calendar** — real-time availability synced with your PMS
- **Guest reviews displayed on-site** — Google Reviews pulled live, not screenshots from 2019
- **Individual property pages** optimized for "[property type] + [city]" searches
- **SSL certificate** — guests will not enter payment info on a site Chrome flags as "Not Secure"

---

## The Window Is Closing

Peak booking season for the Florida Keys runs from December through April. Guests start researching and booking weeks or months in advance. A rental manager who waits until October to modernize their site has already lost a season of direct bookings.

The best time to rebuild was last year. The second best time is now — before your legacy platform becomes the reason your competitor gets the booking instead.
