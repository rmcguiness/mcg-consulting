# Couples Search Google 14 Months Before They Book. If You're Not There, You Don't Exist.

**Published:** March 2026  
**Word Count:** ~850 words

---

The average engaged couple starts searching for a wedding venue 12–18 months before their date. That search almost always starts the same way: they open Google, type something like "Florida Keys wedding venue" or "outdoor wedding ceremony Islamorada," and start clicking through results.

If your venue or planning business isn't ranking in those results, you're not in consideration. Not because couples don't want what you offer — the Keys are one of the most sought-after wedding destinations in the country — but because they don't know you exist. They booked someone else before they ever had a chance to find you.

This is the specific problem that running a wedding business on Facebook and word-of-mouth creates. It works, until it doesn't — and it stops working exactly when someone a year out starts their search cold.

---

## The Facebook-Only Trap

Facebook is good for keeping in touch with past clients, posting vendor shoutouts, and showing recent events to people who already follow you. It is not a discovery engine for couples who don't know your name yet.

Here's what actually happens when a couple from Chicago starts planning a destination Keys wedding. They don't search Facebook. They open Google. They search "wedding venue Overseas Highway," "beachfront ceremony Key West," "intimate wedding planner Marathon Florida." Facebook doesn't show up in those results. Your Facebook page — no matter how many photos you've posted or how many positive comments you've collected — is completely invisible to a Google search.

The same is true for couples browsing on Instagram. They might find you there if they're already deep into research and following wedding hashtags. But first contact almost always happens on Google, and first contact is what determines whether you're on their list or not.

Independent coordinators in Islamorada, boutique ceremony operators in Key Largo, venue owners in Marathon with beautiful waterfront space and zero web presence — all of them are showing up nowhere in the searches that happen 400 times a month from couples who would genuinely love what they're offering.

---

## The Knot and WeddingWire: What That Commission Is Actually Costing You

Plenty of Keys wedding businesses have figured out that they need online visibility, and they've solved it by paying for a profile on The Knot or WeddingWire. This gets them in front of searching couples. The problem is the price.

The Knot and WeddingWire charge vendors **15–30% commission on bookings originated through their platform**, depending on your subscription tier and how the booking is attributed. On a $15,000 wedding package, that's $2,250–$4,500 per booking going to the platform. On a $30,000 all-in coordination package, you're writing checks of $4,500–$9,000 per event to a directory that owns the customer relationship and can raise its rates any time it wants.

The math matters because a properly built website with good local SEO can generate the same inquiries — couples actively searching for Florida Keys wedding services — without the per-booking cut. A couple who finds you through Google and submits an inquiry form on your site is a direct lead. You keep everything. The Knot keeps nothing.

This isn't an argument that The Knot is useless. Early on, or during slow periods, the directory exposure helps. But for an established Keys coordinator or venue doing 15–30 events a year, the commission drag compounds fast. At $3,000 average commission per event across 20 events, that's $60,000 a year going to a platform instead of staying in your business.

## What a Proper Website Does for a Wedding Business

A wedding website isn't a brochure. It's a lead-generation machine — and the economics are different from almost any other hospitality business in the Keys because the booking values are so high.

A coordinator in Key West who lands one additional booking per quarter directly through her website — a booking that would otherwise have gone to a competitor or been discovered through a platform at a 20% commission — covers the cost of a well-built site in a single event. One.

The specific things that move the needle for a Keys wedding site:

**Real photography, front and center.** Couples make emotional decisions. They need to see sunsets over the water, ceremony setups on the beach, reception tables at Hawks Cay or Anne's Beach, the light at dusk over the Atlantic. Text doesn't close wedding clients. Photos do. A site without a full, current photo gallery is a site that doesn't convert.

**Lead capture that actually works.** Not a generic "contact us" form. A structured inquiry form that captures the date, guest count, venue preference, and how they heard about you. Couples who fill out a specific inquiry are serious. A form that makes it easy to inquire is a form that gets used.

**Packages and pricing pages.** Couples planning a Keys destination wedding from out of state need price transparency to budget their trip. If your site doesn't give any pricing guidance, they move to a competitor who does. "Contact us for pricing" is a conversion killer when the couple has fifteen tabs open.

**SEO targeting the searches that matter.** "Florida Keys wedding planner," "outdoor wedding ceremony Key Largo," "wedding venues Islamorada waterfront," "elopement coordinator Key West" — these are specific, high-intent searches from couples who are ready to inquire. Your site needs to rank for them with properly structured pages, local keywords, and a linked Google Business Profile.

**A blog with content brides actually search for.** "Best Florida Keys wedding venues for small ceremonies," "what to know about a Keys destination wedding," "Overseas Highway wedding timeline" — this content ranks on Google and drives planning-stage couples to your site months before they're ready to book. It's how you get in front of them early, before they've already committed to a competitor.

## What to Actually Build

A Keys wedding site that works isn't complicated, but it has to be done right:

- Mobile-first design (brides browse on their phones, always)
- Full gallery with properly captioned real wedding photos
- Package descriptions with ballpark pricing or "starting at" ranges
- A structured inquiry form with availability check
- Vendor partner page (photographers, florists, caterers you trust)
- Blog content targeting planning-stage searches
- Google Business Profile fully claimed, verified, and linked
- Local SEO targeting Key Largo, Islamorada, Marathon, Key West, and "Florida Keys" broadly

The sites that rank and convert aren't fancy. They're focused, fast, and built around what couples actually need when they're 14 months out and filling an inquiry form for the first time.
