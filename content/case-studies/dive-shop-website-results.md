## The Client

Coral Coast Dive Center has operated out of Key Largo since the mid-1990s — long enough to have watched the Keys dive market transform around them and built a loyal following of repeat divers who come back every year. The operation runs two dive boats daily, offers the full suite of PADI courses from Discover Scuba through Divemaster, and rents a complete gear inventory including BCDs, regulators, wetsuits, and underwater cameras.

The reviews reflected all of that. Over 300 TripAdvisor reviews averaging 4.6 stars. Consistent praise for the dive masters, the condition of the equipment, and the owner's personal involvement in every trip. Real credibility built over three decades.

The website reflected none of it.

---

## The Problem

When owner Marcus Delgado reached out to MCG, the coral-coast-dive.com site had been live — barely — since around 2007. It was a table-based HTML layout, hand-coded at build time by a web designer who hadn't been involved since the first Bush administration. The copyright footer read "© 2007–2026," which was technically accurate but felt more like a museum timestamp than a business credential.

Specific problems:

- **No mobile responsiveness.** The site rendered as a fixed-width desktop layout on every device. On an iPhone, the navigation text was 8 pixels tall. The "Book a Trip" link — which went to a third-party listing page — required a two-finger pinch and a prayer.
- **No online booking.** Every dive trip booking required a phone call. Trip inquiries came in by email and were answered manually, usually within 24 hours. Bookings made outside of business hours simply didn't happen.
- **No gear rental pricing on the site.** Tourists comparing Coral Coast with Key Largo competitors had to call to find out what a full equipment rental cost. The ones who didn't call didn't book.
- **No certification course funnel.** The courses page was a single paragraph listing available certifications with no pricing, no schedule, and no way to enroll. Every certification booking started with a phone call.
- **A photo gallery with seven images, two of which were stock.** For a business selling an underwater experience at one of the world's most famous dive destinations.

Marcus knew the site was outdated. What he didn't know was the specific dollar amount attached to the problem. When we mapped out his booking flow against local competitor sites, it became clear: he was losing 30–40 after-hours inquiries per week simply because there was no way to capture them.

---

## What We Built

The goal was a fast, mobile-first site that ranked for Key Largo dive searches, communicated everything a diver needed to decide, and let them book without picking up a phone.

**Full rebuild** on a modern CMS with mobile-first design:

- **FareHarbor integration** across all bookings — two-tank reef dives, half-day snorkel trips, Discover Scuba experiences, advanced specialty courses, and gear-only rentals each got their own booking flow. A customer can now select a date, choose the number of divers, enter certification level, pay a deposit, and receive a confirmation email without any staff involvement. The live availability calendar embedded in the site updates in real time.

- **Dedicated certification course pages.** Each PADI course — Open Water, Advanced Open Water, Rescue Diver, Divemaster, specialty courses — now has a standalone page with the full course description, prerequisites, what's included, schedule, and pricing. This structure alone created 8 new indexable pages targeting high-intent search terms like "PADI Open Water Key Largo" and "rescue diver certification Florida Keys."

- **Gear rental pricing page.** Full rental menu with individual item and full-kit pricing, wetsuit sizing chart, and a note about camera rental availability. The result was immediate: tourists who previously bounced off the site because they couldn't find prices are now reading the gear page and booking with it open.

- **Photo gallery.** A half-day shoot produced 55 images: coral reef formations, divers on the Spiegel Grove, the dive boat at sunset, a group of Open Water students getting briefed on the dock, a dive master pointing out a spotted eagle ray. Every image compressed and optimized. Each trip page has 6–10 images directly from Coral Coast's operation.

- **Google Business Profile overhaul.** New photos, updated hours, added FareHarbor booking link directly in the profile, rewrote the business description with targeted local keywords, and solicited a batch of fresh Google reviews from recent customers. Within 60 days, Coral Coast was in the top 3 on Google Maps for "scuba diving Key Largo."

- **TripAdvisor review widget.** 300+ reviews at 4.6 stars are now displayed on the homepage. The social proof that was previously siloed on a third-party platform now shows up the moment a tourist lands on the site.

## The Results (120 Days Post-Launch)

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Direct online bookings (% of total) | 12% | 47% |
| After-hours bookings captured | ~0/week | 18–22/week |
| Mobile bounce rate | 81% | 29% |
| Mobile page load time | 9.4 seconds | 1.8 seconds |
| Certification course bookings | Rolling phone inquiries | Sold out 3–4 weeks in advance |
| Gear rental inquiries | Call-based only | Up 50% via site contact form |
| Google Maps rank: "scuba diving Key Largo" | Not in top 20 | Position 2 |

The 35% increase in overall online bookings came from two places: tourists who found Coral Coast through Google (where they were previously invisible), and after-hours bookings from customers who wanted to book at 9pm and could now do so. Prior to the rebuild, Marcus estimates he was losing 15–20 trip bookings per week simply because those customers had no self-serve option.

The certification course result was the one Marcus didn't fully anticipate. The dedicated course pages, with clear pricing and a live FareHarbor enrollment link, changed how tourists planned their Keys trip. Instead of calling to "ask about courses," people were now enrolling weeks in advance. For the first time since he opened the business, Marcus found himself managing waitlists for Open Water courses during peak season.

## Why It Worked

Key Largo is not a market where it's hard to attract dive customers. It's the Dive Capital of the World — the demand is there, constant, driven by millions of tourists per year. The question is never whether tourists want to dive. It's whether they can find your operation and book it before they find someone else.

Coral Coast had the reputation. The boats, the staff, the certifications, the 30-year track record. They just had a front door that sent people away before they ever got inside.
