# Two Keys Businesses, Two Website Overhauls, One Pattern: Direct Revenue That Wasn't There Before

---

## Case Study 1: Hammock Cove Massage & Wellness, Marathon, FL

**Business:** Hammock Cove Massage & Wellness  
**Location:** Marathon, FL (MM 49)  
**Industry:** Therapeutic Massage / Wellness  
**Timeline:** 2-week build + 90 days tracked post-launch

---

### Hero Stats

| 40% more direct bookings | $800/month in new gift card revenue | Fully booked 2 weeks in advance |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| in the first 90 days | from a gift card store that didn't exist before | during peak season (first time) |

---

### The Client

Hammock Cove Massage & Wellness is a therapeutic practice in Marathon run by owner and licensed massage therapist Dana Ruiz. Dana has been practicing in the Keys for over a decade, with deep expertise in deep tissue, hot stone, and couples massage. Her long-term clients drive from Key Largo and Big Pine to see her. Reviews on Facebook and TripAdvisor consistently describe her work as some of the best massage in the Keys — and mean it.

Her digital presence when she first called MCG: a Facebook business page with 1,100 followers and a TripAdvisor listing that linked to her phone number. That was it.

---

### The Problem

Everything at Hammock Cove ran through the phone. Existing clients called or texted. Tourists who happened to find the TripAdvisor listing called, if they were persistent enough to dial instead of just moving on. New customers — the tourists doing a Google search from their rental house at night — didn't find Dana at all.

The specific issues:

- **Zero Google presence.** Searches for "massage Marathon FL," "couples massage Florida Keys," or "therapeutic massage Marathon" returned competitors, resort spas, and directory listings. Hammock Cove wasn't indexed anywhere that a search engine could surface.
- **Phone-only booking.** Every appointment required a direct conversation. During peak season, this created a bottleneck: Dana was treating clients while texts piled up unanswered. Some of those texts turned into bookings. Many didn't.
- **No gift card revenue stream.** Tourism-driven businesses in the Keys have a natural gift market — visitors buying something for themselves or to bring home. With no website and no e-commerce, Dana had no way to offer gift cards to anyone who wasn't standing in front of her.
- **No ability to capture pre-arrival bookings.** Couples planning a Keys getaway research everything two weeks in advance. Without an online presence, Hammock Cove was invisible to this entire segment.

---

### What We Built

A clean, mobile-first website focused on three things: being found on Google, making booking frictionless, and selling gift cards.

**Acuity Scheduling integration** — a live booking calendar embedded in the site let clients select a service, choose a date and time, and pay a deposit without calling. The system sends automatic confirmation and reminder emails. Dana could now take bookings at midnight from a tourist in a Key West Airbnb planning their Marathon day.

**Gift card store via Square** — a single-page shop with digital gift card denominations in $50, $100, and $150 increments, integrated into the same checkout flow as appointments. Set it up once; it runs continuously.

**Google Business Profile optimization** — verified profile, 18 real photos, hours, services listed, booking link added directly in the profile. Within 45 days, Hammock Cove appeared in the Google Maps Local Pack for "massage Marathon FL."

**Service pages with clear pricing** — Swedish, deep tissue, hot stone, couples massage, and prenatal massage each got their own page with duration, description, and price listed. No "call for rates." No ambiguity.

---

### The Results (90 Days Post-Launch)

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Direct online bookings (% of total) | ~0% (all phone/text) | 40% of all bookings |
| After-hours bookings | 0/week | 9–12/week |
| Gift card revenue | $0/month | $800/month average |
| Peak season availability | Spots open mid-week | Fully booked 2 weeks out |
| Google Maps rank: "massage Marathon FL" | Not indexed | Top 3 |

The 40% direct booking shift represented appointments Dana was genuinely not capturing before — the tourist couple who searched at 9pm, the visitor who found the TripAdvisor listing, clicked through to the site, and booked without ever picking up the phone. The gift card revenue was almost entirely incremental: $800/month from buyers who would have had no way to purchase without the store.

---

### Dana's Take

> "I was fully booked by word-of-mouth, but I had no idea how many people were looking for me on Google and not finding me. The gift cards surprised me the most — I had no idea that was money I was missing. Now I have regulars who buy them for every holiday and people I've never met buying them as Keys trip gifts. That whole revenue stream just didn't exist before."

## Case Study 2: Tidewater Events & Ceremonies, Florida Keys

**Business:** Tidewater Events & Ceremonies  
**Owner:** Renée Castillo  
**Location:** Islamorada, FL (serving Keys-wide)  
**Industry:** Destination Wedding Planning & Coordination  
**Timeline:** 3-week build + 6 months tracked post-launch

---

### Hero Stats

| Inquiry form conversions tripled | Now booked 18 months out | 60% of new leads arrive through Google |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| within the first 60 days of launch | for the first time in 7 years of business | not through The Knot or WeddingWire |

---

### The Client

Renée Castillo has been coordinating Keys weddings since 2019. She knows every waterfront ceremony location between Key Largo and Key West, has relationships with every reliable vendor, and has a gift for managing the logistics of destination weddings where the couple is planning from 1,200 miles away. Her referral pipeline kept her busy — but inconsistently, with slow stretches in spring and fall when the referral flow dried up.

Her website when she first reached out to MCG: a Wix site with a single homepage containing one photo, a short paragraph about wedding planning, and a "contact me" link. No gallery. No packages. No pricing guidance. No sense of her personality or her work.

---

### The Problem

The Wix site was doing exactly one thing: confirming to people who had already heard of Renée that she was still in business. It wasn't generating inquiries on its own, wasn't ranking for any wedding-related searches, and wasn't giving couples from out of state any reason to choose Renée over a competitor whose site showed actual weddings she had coordinated.

Specific issues:

- **Zero Google ranking.** Searches for "Florida Keys wedding planner," "wedding coordinator Islamorada," and "destination wedding Keys" returned The Knot, WeddingWire, and a handful of competitors with real sites. Renée appeared nowhere.
- **No photo gallery.** Renée had beautiful photography from dozens of weddings. None of it was on her site.
- **No package or pricing information.** Destination couples from out of state budget 12–18 months ahead. A site with no pricing guidance sends those couples to competitors who give them a number to plan around.
- **Platform dependency.** Renée had a WeddingWire listing that generated some inquiries, but at an 18–22% commission on attributed bookings. On a $15,000 full-coordination package, that's $2,700–$3,300 per booking going to the platform.
- **No inquiry form structure.** The contact page was a generic email link. There was no way for an interested couple to provide date, guest count, venue preference, or any other qualifying information before the first conversation.

---

### What We Built

A conversion-focused portfolio site built around Renée's actual work, designed to attract couples in the research phase and make them confident enough to inquire.

**Portfolio pages with real wedding photography** — six dedicated "Featured Weddings" pages with venue, date, guest count, and a full photo gallery from each event. These pages serve two purposes: they show prospective couples what their wedding could look like, and they create indexable content that targets location-specific searches.

**Package pages with "starting at" pricing** — Full Coordination, Month-of Coordination, Day-of Coordination, and Elopement packages each got a page with a scope description, typical inclusions, and a "starting at" price range. This gave destination couples a framework to budget without locking Renée into a fixed quote she'd have to walk back.

**Structured inquiry form** — replacing the email link with a form that captures: event date (or preferred timeframe), estimated guest count, ceremony location preference, reception venue (if known), how they heard about Renée, and a free-form message. Inquiries from couples who completed the form were substantially more qualified than cold email contacts.

**Blog content targeting destination wedding searches** — five posts targeting planning-phase searches ("what to know about a Florida Keys destination wedding," "best months for an outdoor ceremony in the Keys," "how far out to book a Keys wedding coordinator"). These now rank on the first page for several low-competition long-tail queries and bring couples to the site in the research phase — months before they're ready to book.

**Google Business Profile** — verified, optimized, linked to the site. Added 20 event photos. Within 90 days, Tidewater appeared in the local pack for "wedding coordinator Islamorada."

---

### The Results (6 Months Post-Launch)

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Monthly inquiry form submissions | 2–3 | 8–10 |
| Source of new inquiries | 80% referral / 20% WeddingWire | 60% organic Google / 25% referral / 15% WeddingWire |
| Average booking lead time | 6–9 months | 16–18 months |
| WeddingWire commission spend | ~$2,400/month (peak) | ~$600/month |
| Bookings attributable to site | 0 | 9 in first 6 months |

The tripling of inquiry form conversions came from two changes: destination couples now finding Renée through Google searches (where she previously didn't exist), and a structured inquiry form that made it easy to take the next step. The shift to 18-month lead times meant peak season dates were filling up in January for the following fall — a dynamic that had never existed before.

---

### Renée's Take

> "I was completely invisible on Google. I knew that — I just didn't realize how many couples were searching and never finding me. The inquiry form made a huge difference too. I used to get emails that just said 'I'm getting married in the Keys, can you help?' and I'd spend three emails just figuring out the basics. Now people come through with a date, a venue, a guest count, and a real message. They're serious. The conversations close faster."
