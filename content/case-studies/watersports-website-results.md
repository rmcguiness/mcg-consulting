## The Client

Tidal Wave Watersports has run kayak rentals, paddleboard tours, snorkel trips, and backcountry boat rentals out of Big Pine Key for eight years. It's a small, owner-operated business — two full-time staff in peak season, a fleet of 12 kayaks, six paddleboards, three rental boats, and a guided backcountry eco-tour that runs twice daily.

The reviews were strong: 4.6 stars across 180+ Google reviews, with guests consistently mentioning the guide's knowledge of the National Key Deer Refuge and the quality of the snorkel gear. The product was solid. The digital presence was not.

---

## The Problem

When owner Lisa Cartwright first reached out to MCG, her entire online presence was a Facebook business page and a GetMyBoat listing. She had a website URL — it pointed to a Wix site she'd built herself in 2020 — but it hadn't been updated since 2021 and the mobile layout had degraded to the point where the booking form was inaccessible on most phones.

The specific issues:

- **Facebook-first, Google-invisible.** The Wix site wasn't optimized for any local search terms. Searches for "kayak rental Big Pine Key," "snorkel tour Lower Keys," and "boat rental National Key Deer Refuge" returned competitors and OTA listings. Tidal Wave didn't appear in the first three pages of results.
- **GetMyBoat taking 25% per booking.** Lisa had leaned into GetMyBoat to pick up the online bookings her broken site couldn't capture. It worked — about 35% of her bookings came through the platform. But at 20–25% blended commission on a $120 kayak rental or a $180 snorkel tour, she was losing $24–$45 per reservation. Across a full peak season, that was roughly $18,000 walking out the door in platform fees.
- **Mobile site broken.** The Wix template looked fine on a desktop but had collapsed on mobile. The navigation menu covered the hero image. The "Book Now" button linked to a GetMyBoat page rather than a direct booking form. On iPhone, the pricing table required horizontal scrolling. Mobile bounce rate was 76%.
- **No photo gallery.** The site had four photos — two of a generic kayak on water, one stock image of a snorkeler, and a blurry action shot from 2019. For a business selling an outdoor visual experience in one of the most photogenic environments in the country, this was a significant conversion problem.

---

## What We Built

The goal was simple: a fast, mobile-first site that shows up on Google, shows real photography, and lets tourists book without leaving the page.

**Full site rebuild** in Webflow, optimized for mobile from the ground up:

- **FareHarbor integration** — each activity (kayak rental, paddleboard rental, guided eco-tour, snorkel trip, backcountry boat rental) has its own page with a live FareHarbor availability calendar embedded directly. Tourists pick their activity, select a date and time, choose group size, and pay a deposit. Total time: under three minutes. The system captures bookings around the clock — including the 8pm–midnight window that Lisa was losing entirely.
- **Google Maps optimization** — we updated and fully completed the Google Business Profile, added the new website URL, uploaded 22 fresh photos, wrote an optimized business description targeting Big Pine Key and the Lower Keys, and added FareHarbor as a booking link directly in the profile. Combined with the new site's local SEO structure (LocalBusiness schema, targeted title tags, location-specific page content), Tidal Wave moved from invisible to #3 in the Google Maps pack for "boat rental Big Pine Key" within 60 days.
- **Photo gallery** — a half-day shoot on the water produced 40+ usable images: kayakers in the mangrove channels, paddleboarders on flat water at sunrise, a snorkeler over a reef, a guided tour group with the guide explaining something. All compressed to WebP and organized by activity. Each activity page has 6–8 real photos from the actual operation.
- **Review integration** — 180+ Google reviews at 4.6 stars now display on the homepage via a live widget. Social proof visible within the first scroll.
- **Activity pages built to convert** — each page includes the full details a tourist needs to say yes: duration, what's included, skill level required, minimum age, what to wear, where to park, and transparent pricing. No "call for rates." No vague language.

## The Results (90 Days Post-Launch)

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Direct online bookings (% of total) | 18% | 58% |
| GetMyBoat bookings (% of total) | 35% | 8% |
| Mobile bounce rate | 76% | 31% |
| Mobile page load time | 8.2 seconds | 1.9 seconds |
| Google Maps rank: "boat rental Big Pine Key" | Not in top 20 | Position 3 |
| After-hours bookings captured | ~0/month | 22/month |
| OTA fees saved (annualized) | — | ~$18,000 |

The 40% increase in direct bookings represents a shift that came from two places: tourists who previously found Tidal Wave on GetMyBoat now find them directly on Google, and after-hours bookings that previously went to competitors — or to GetMyBoat by default — are now captured directly through FareHarbor on the site.

The $18,000 in annual OTA fee savings is calculated from the reduction in GetMyBoat booking volume, multiplied by the average 22% commission rate on Lisa's product mix. The site build paid for itself within the first peak season.

---

## Why It Worked

The Lower Keys watersports market is not oversaturated. For most activities — kayak rentals, guided eco-tours, snorkel trips near the reef — there are only a handful of operators between Marathon and Key West. Tourists searching for these activities are high-intent, low-friction customers. They want to do the thing. They just need a business they can find and book.

Tidal Wave had the experience, the reviews, and the location. What they didn't have was a functional front door on Google. Once the site was live, loading fast, ranking locally, and showing real photos, the bookings followed — because the demand was already there.
