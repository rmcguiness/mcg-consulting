## 1. The Fish House — Key Largo

**Contact:** Chef/Owner (Key Largo, FL)
**Website:** thefishhouse.com
**Audit date:** March 13, 2026

### Executive Summary

The Fish House has been a Key Largo institution for years — fresh-off-the-boat seafood, waterfront views, and a loyal local following. But your website is costing you tourist covers every single night. Based on our audit, we estimate you're losing **15–25 covers per week** from tourists who search for Key Largo restaurants on their phones and never find you. At an average check of $45/person, that's roughly **$2,700–$4,500/month left on the table.**

### The 5 Issues Costing You Money

#### 1. No Online Reservation System — $1,800–$3,000/month in lost covers
Your website says "Call for reservations." Here's the problem:
- 70% of your tourist traffic is planning dinner from their hotel room at 9–11pm
- Your phone goes to voicemail after close
- A tourist who can't book immediately will book the next restaurant that lets them — tonight
- Industry data shows 55–65% of diners who encounter a "call to reserve" page abandon and book elsewhere
- At your average cover, even 10 lost covers/week = $1,800/month minimum

#### 2. Google Business Profile Is Underoptimized
Your Google listing has fewer than 20 photos, and most are user-uploaded (low quality, bad lighting). The menu link goes to a PDF that takes 6 seconds to load on mobile. Your seasonal hours aren't updated — Google shows your off-season hours during peak tourist season. Restaurants with optimized profiles get 5x more direction requests. Yours is leaving discovery traffic to competitors with better listings.

#### 3. Menu Is a PDF — Google Can't Read It
Your menu is uploaded as a scanned PDF. Google can't index PDF menu items, which means when someone searches "stone crab Key Largo" or "fresh catch Key Largo," your restaurant doesn't appear — even though you serve exactly that. A text-based, mobile-friendly menu page would capture dozens of these long-tail searches per month.

#### 4. Mobile Experience Is Slow and Dated
Your site loads in 8+ seconds on mobile. The layout isn't responsive — text is tiny, navigation requires pinch-to-zoom, and the reservation phone number is buried in the footer. 68% of tourists browse restaurants on their phones. A site that doesn't work on mobile is a site that doesn't work.

#### 5. No Review Strategy
You have 300+ Google reviews with a strong rating, but none appear on your website. No review widget, no testimonials section, no recent quotes. Tourists researching restaurants compare review presence on the website itself — not just Google. And you're not responding to negative reviews on Google, which signals to both Google and potential diners that you're not engaged.

### What We'd Build for The Fish House

1. **Mobile-first responsive website** — clean, modern design that loads in under 2 seconds on any device. Waterfront photography optimized for mobile (WebP, lazy-loaded).

2. **Text-based menu with search-optimized structure** — each section (appetizers, raw bar, entrées, drinks) as indexable HTML. When someone Googles "Key Largo lobster dinner," your site shows up.

3. **Direct reservation widget** — Resy or custom booking form embedded on every page. Date, time, party size, confirmation email. Works at 11pm when your phone doesn't.

4. **Google Business Profile optimization** — 50+ professional food photos, accurate seasonal hours, menu link to your new text-based menu page, response templates for reviews.

5. **Live Google review widget** — your best reviews displayed on the homepage and menu pages. Social proof visible the moment someone lands on your site.

6. **Local SEO foundation** — optimized title tags, meta descriptions, and Restaurant schema markup targeting "Key Largo seafood restaurant," "waterfront dining Key Largo," and "fresh fish Key Largo."

Tourists searching "Key West seafood restaurant" find the Duval Street spots first because those restaurants invest in online visibility. A simple "walk-ins welcome — check current wait times" widget or online ordering for takeout would capture the planners who currently skip you.

#### 3. Website Is Functional but Not Optimized
Your site works, but it's not built for Google. No schema markup, thin meta descriptions, and page titles that don't target the searches tourists actually make ("Stock Island restaurant," "Key West waterfront seafood," "best hogfish Key West"). You have an incredible story — fresh fish from the boats docked next to you, a true Keys waterfront bar, the kind of place travel writers love. But your website tells none of it. 3. **Story-driven content** — an "About" page that tells the real Hogfish story. Where the fish comes from, how long you've been on Stock Island, why locals eat here. Content that ranks for "best hidden gem Key West" and "Stock Island seafood."

4. **Google Business optimization** — professional photos of the waterfront, the food, the bar. Updated description emphasizing fresh-off-the-boat seafood and the locals' favorite angle. Active review responses.
